## Nick Cammarata says most AI neurons are legible, roon calls it historic, skeptics push back, and Sauers shows models can detect probes

*Ongoing · 3 days* — Former OpenAI interpretability researcher Nick Cammarata claimed agentic tools reveal that most neurons in trillion-parameter models are understandable. roon hailed it as a turning point, while Eliezer Yudkowsky, Leo Gao and others warned of over-updating, and the debate carried into the weekend.

- Fri: Cammarata said he once found about 2% of neurons legible but now finds 'the vast majority' are, calling it a possible 'bitter lesson for interpretability'; roon wrote 'neurons are already clean!' and said alignment could become a 'normal science.'
- Fri: Yudkowsky rejected the 'normal science' framing, and Leo Gao and jankulveit called the reaction an over-update that needs rigorous testing.
- Sat: EigenGender joked that what matters is the paper 'has the right vibes,' and Zvi Mowshowitz replied that this 'IS EXACTLY THE WRONG VIBE.'
- Sun: Liv asked whether monosemantic neurons would cap how many entities a model knows, as Sauers showed Qwen3-8B can learn to detect when its activations are being read by a probe.

**How these connect:** All three days revolve around Cammarata's claim that neurons are legible: EigenGender mocked the vibes around his paper, and Sunday's neuron-semantics debate (Liv's question) responded directly to it alongside Sauers' probe-detection thread.
