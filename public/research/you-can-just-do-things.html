You can just do things

“You can just do things” is about agency: a reminder that you have it, which is not most people's default belief. It showed up during lockdown in the part of Twitter known as TPOT.

TPOT is the part of Twitter between rationalist blogs, meditation, therapy, AI research. We think it is unusually generative as a scene, and this phrase is one example. In five years it went from encouraging generic agency among online nerds, to a tech startup mantra, to being tweeted by the president of El Salvador and the CTO of the Department of War.

My friend Guy asked me to map how this meme spread and changed. This is that map, drawn from tweets. It is also a demonstration of what the Community Archive is for: tracing where ideas come from.

Takeaways

  • It started in TPOT. Almost nobody said it before 2020, and through 2023, Community Archive members wrote of all uses.
  • Then it spread outward: first to tech, then politics. The closer an account sits to archive members, the earlier it picked the phrase up. Use took off in October 2024 and peaked in February 2025.

Wording

Two variants, “things” and “stuff”, and one won

Inside TPOT the phrase came in two wordings from the start. In August 2022 Nat Sharpe posted a video captioned “you can just do stuff”, eight members retweeted it, and TPOT began calling it “Nat's Law”. Over the following year “stuff” was the slightly more common form, and ten weeks after the video Sam Altman used it too. When use took off in late 2024 it was “things” that spread, and “stuff” fell to one use in twenty.

Share of uses saying “stuff” rather than “things”, by year

“do stuff”“do things”

Before the video (Aug 29, 2022): 13 of 41 uses said “stuff”. In the year after: 52 of 96. In 2025: 5%. Counting only the exact sentence “you can just do things” or “you can just do stuff” gives the same picture: 55% “stuff” in 2022, 39% in 2023, 14% in 2024 and 4% in 2025.

Before TPOT

Amanda Palmer, 2015

The earliest use in the archive is a 2015 tweet that quotes Amanda Palmer's tumblr.

…that thinking that is what makes life bearable, when you realize you can just Do Things.Amanda Palmer, Oct 28, 2015 · tumblr

It has the same register as TPOT's first uses: permission, not ambition. We found no recorded path from it into TPOT's 2020 uses, and the sentence is ordinary enough English to have been coined more than once. What argues against many independent coinages is how rare it was: our greater-TPOT data holds between one and five million tweets a year for 2017 to 2019 and not one slogan use.

Method and limits

What this can and can't show

  • Two sources. The counts and Figures 1 to 3 come from the greater-TPOT data (to Aug 24, 2026). The quotes after that date and the 2015 and 2017 tweets come from the Community Archive (to Nov 10, 2026). Together they hold distinct uses.
  • Everything here is near TPOT. Both sources are built outward from archive members. “First” means first in these sources, not first on Twitter, and uses by people far from this scene are missing at every date.
  • Absence of a link is not evidence of none. Language spreads by reading. We only see replies, mentions and quotes.
  • The fall after early 2025 is a fall in rate. Raw counts stay high because the database holds twice as many tweets for 2026 as for 2025. The rate falls inside every ring, including members, so it is not only an effect of who was added.
  • Likes are as last observed, not final, and follower counts are as of each tweet.

How this was made. Research and drafting by AI agents (Claude) directed and checked by the archive's maintainer.
